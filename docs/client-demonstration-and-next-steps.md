# Jamy Fashion's UG — Client Demonstration and Next Steps

## Purpose of this document

Use this guide to present the sample website confidently, collect the information needed for the full project, and agree on payment and scope before doing more development.

The sample is available at:

**https://jamyfashionsug.syntaxsystems.co**

## 1. Before the demonstration

Prepare the following before meeting or calling the client:

- Open the website on both a phone and a laptop.
- Confirm that the internet connection is stable.
- Open one dress and one hijab product page in separate tabs.
- Confirm that WhatsApp is available on the demonstration device.
- Keep this guide and a place for notes nearby.
- Avoid presenting the sample as a finished online store. It is a visual and functional concept for the proposed full website.

## 2. Suggested opening

> I used the logo, brand colours and product photographs you shared to create a sample of how Jamy Fashion's UG could work as a digital shop. The goal is to let customers browse your collection professionally and contact you on WhatsApp about the exact item they want.

## 3. Demonstration flow

### Step 1: Show the homepage

Start at the top of the homepage and explain:

> This is the first impression customers receive when they open your website. It introduces Jamy Fashion's UG as a polished modest-fashion brand and immediately gives customers options to browse the collection or order through WhatsApp.

Point out:

- The Jamy Fashion's UG branding, logo, navy, gold and cream colour palette.
- The main “Shop collection” and “Order on WhatsApp” actions.
- The mobile-friendly navigation.
- The clear focus on modest fashion and everyday confidence.

### Step 2: Show product categories

Scroll to “Shop by category”.

> Customers can quickly choose whether they want to browse casual dresses or hijabs instead of receiving many unrelated photographs in one WhatsApp conversation.

Explain that the full website can include more approved categories such as:

- Casual dresses and abayas.
- Hijabs.
- Bags and footwear.
- Accessories.
- Custom outfits.

### Step 3: Show new arrivals and products

Scroll to “New arrivals”.

> Each item is presented consistently with a clear photograph, product name, price and short description. The current names, prices and descriptions are sample content and will be replaced or confirmed using your real catalogue.

Point out that customers can browse without repeatedly requesting individual pictures.

### Step 4: Open a product page

Select one product card.

> Every product can have its own page with more photographs, price, available colours, sizes, description and important details. This also gives each product a link that you can share directly on WhatsApp, TikTok or another social platform.

Demonstrate:

- The product photograph gallery.
- Product name and price.
- Colour and size information.
- Product description and details.
- Related products.

### Step 5: Demonstrate WhatsApp ordering

Select “Ask about this item on WhatsApp”.

Show the client the prepared message before sending it:

> Hello Jamy Fashion's UG, I am interested in the [product name] priced at UGX [price]. Is it available in my size/colour?
>
> View product: [product page link]

Explain:

> The customer does not need to type the product name or send a screenshot. The message already contains the item, price and direct product link, so you immediately know what they are asking about.

Do not send the demonstration message unless the client wants you to.

### Step 6: Show the brand and social sections

Point out the brand story, contact details and TikTok area.

> These sections help customers understand and trust the business while giving them another way to follow the brand. The temporary TikTok link will be replaced with your official profile.

### Step 7: Show the mobile experience

Open the sample on a phone or reduce the browser width.

> Most customers will probably open the link from WhatsApp, TikTok or another social app on their phones, so the website has been designed to work clearly on smaller screens.

Show the mobile menu, product cards, product page and floating WhatsApp button.

## 4. Questions to ask after the demonstration

Allow the client to respond before discussing technical details.

Ask:

1. Does this visual style represent how you want customers to see Jamy Fashion's UG?
2. Which parts of the sample do you like most?
3. Is there anything about the colours, wording or layout that should change?
4. Which product categories should appear in the first official version?
5. Should the first version continue using WhatsApp ordering, or do you want a shopping cart and online payment quoted separately?
6. Who will provide and approve product names, prices, descriptions, sizes, colours and availability?
7. How often do you expect products and prices to change?
8. Would you prefer to send updates to the developer, or pay for an admin area where you can manage products yourself?
9. What delivery areas, pickup options and delivery charges should customers see?
10. Do you already own a preferred domain name?

Record the answers. Do not promise features or deadlines that are not included in the agreed scope.

## 5. Proposed full-project scope

The first paid version can include:

- Final responsive homepage based on the approved sample.
- Confirmed brand content and official social links.
- Approved product categories.
- Real product catalogue with photographs, prices and descriptions.
- Individual product pages with shareable links.
- Product-specific WhatsApp enquiries.
- Sizes, colours and availability information supplied by the client.
- Brand story, contact information, ordering guidance and delivery information.
- Basic search-engine and social-sharing metadata.
- Production deployment, HTTPS and final quality checks.
- Two reasonable revision rounds before final approval.

The following are separate features unless added to the written quotation:

- Customer accounts.
- Shopping cart and checkout.
- Mobile Money or card payments.
- Automated delivery pricing.
- Live inventory and order management.
- Admin dashboard for managing products.
- Professional product photography, copywriting or logo redesign.
- Paid domain, hosting, email or third-party service fees.
- Ongoing maintenance and unlimited future product updates.

## 6. Information required from the client

### Business and brand

- Confirmed business name and preferred spelling.
- Best available logo files, preferably transparent PNG or vector format.
- Final brand story or approval to refine the supplied story.
- Official WhatsApp number, telephone number and email address.
- Official TikTok and other social-profile links.
- Operating hours and business location, if it should be public.

### Product catalogue

For every product:

- Product name.
- Category.
- Selling price in UGX.
- Clear photographs.
- Short description.
- Available sizes.
- Available colours.
- Fabric or material details.
- Current availability or stock status.
- Whether the item is new, popular or featured.

### Ordering and delivery

- Delivery areas.
- Delivery charges or how charges are calculated.
- Pickup options.
- Expected delivery time.
- Payment methods currently accepted.
- Exchange, return or cancellation policy.
- Custom-order process and expected completion time.

### Technical and ownership decisions

- Preferred final domain name.
- Whether the client needs an admin area.
- Who will maintain products after launch.
- Whether hosting and maintenance should be included in a recurring plan.

## 7. Proposed payment structure

Confirm the final quotation before presenting these figures.

If the agreed full-project price remains **UGX 850,000**, use this structure:

- **Booking deposit — 50%: UGX 425,000**
  - Confirms the project booking.
  - Starts full development and content preparation.
  - Is due before additional project work begins.
- **Final balance — 50%: UGX 425,000**
  - Due after the agreed scope and revision rounds are approved.
  - Must be cleared before final handover, credentials transfer or official launch.

Domain registration, paid hosting, premium services and newly requested features should be listed separately unless the quotation specifically includes them.

### Suggested payment wording

> The full website project is UGX 850,000 based on the agreed scope. A 50% deposit of UGX 425,000 confirms the booking and allows me to begin the complete project. The remaining UGX 425,000 is paid after the agreed work and revisions are approved, before final handover and launch. Any feature outside the written scope will first be discussed and quoted separately.

## 8. Approval and change control

Before continuing, obtain written confirmation of:

- The approved scope.
- Total project price.
- Deposit amount and payment method.
- Expected delivery period.
- Number of revision rounds.
- Content responsibilities.
- Features that are explicitly excluded.

Use a written message, signed proposal or invoice so both sides have the same understanding.

When the client requests something new after approval:

1. Confirm the request in writing.
2. Explain whether it changes the cost or timeline.
3. Obtain approval before implementing it.

## 9. Suggested delivery timeline

A reasonable starting estimate is **10–14 working days after the deposit and complete content are received**. Confirm the actual timeline after reviewing the final number of products and requested features.

The timeline pauses when required content, feedback or approval is outstanding from the client.
